## Design Context

### Users
A personal LifeOS for one technically capable user. Workout tracking happens primarily on a phone during a gym session, often with unreliable connectivity; review and setup also happen on a desktop. The core job is to record sets quickly without losing focus, then retain a trustworthy history.

### Brand Personality
Optimistic, kinetic, and precise. LifeOS should feel like a piece of premium consumer fitness hardware: energetic enough to motivate, calm enough to read mid-workout, and always data-led.

### Aesthetic Direction
Retro-futurist fitness instrumentation with Swiss graphic discipline. Reinterpret the supplied reference rather than copying its layout: a cool mist-lilac canvas, near-white modules, graphite type, and a vivid vermilion-orange signal color. Use IBM Plex Sans for the interface and Doto selectively for hero metrics, timers, counters, and compact status labels. Combine structured whitespace with asymmetric modular cards, compact badges, flat pie/ring graphics, thin outlined icons, and bold scale shifts. Use controlled 16px card radii and 12px field radii; reserve full pills for navigation, badges, and compact controls. Surfaces stay flat—no gradients, glass, glow, or decorative shadows.

### Design Principles
1. Let one oversized data point establish each screen's hierarchy.
2. Reserve the dot-matrix face for numbers and short instrument-like labels; never use it for body copy or forms.
3. Use vermilion for primary action and live/completed state, graphite for navigation, and cool neutrals for structure.
4. Build layouts from a small family of restrained modules: 16px cards, 12px fields, compact pills, and open whitespace.
5. Keep review screens open and editorial, but make active workout tracking dense, edge-to-edge on mobile, thumb-friendly, and immediately legible.
6. Treat offline data as the source of truth; connectivity is an enhancement, never a blocker.
7. Preserve the same visual language on desktop by widening the composition, not by inflating mobile components.
